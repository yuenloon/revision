Core idea

A manometer converts a height of fluid into pressure. Walk through it one fluid layer at a time: going down in a fluid column adds pressure (ρgh), going up subtracts it.

P = Patm + ρgh  (simple manometer)
Pabs = Patm + Pgage
Multi-fluid: start at one end, add/subtract each ρgh term until you reach the other end.

Worked examples

Simple manometer (tank pressure)

Q: A manometer measures a tank's pressure. The fluid has SG=0.85, the column height is 55 cm, and atmospheric pressure is 96 kPa. Determine the absolute pressure.
SG=0.85 fluid, column=55 cm, Patm=96 kPa
  1. Density: ρ = SG × ρwater = 0.85 × 1000 = 850 kg/m³
  2. Formula: P = Patm + ρgh
  3. Substitute: P = 96 + (850)(9.81)(0.55)/1000 = 96 + 4.586 = 100.6 kPa
  4. Gauge pressure: Pgage = P − Patm = 100.6 − 96 = 4.6 kPa
Absolute = 100.6 kPa, Gauge = 4.6 kPa

Multi-fluid manometer (air pressure in tank)

Q: A tank sits at 1400 m altitude, where Patm=85.6 kPa. A multi-fluid manometer reads h₁=0.1 m water, h₂=0.2 m oil (SG=0.85), h₃=0.35 m mercury (SG=13.6). Determine the air pressure in the tank.
Altitude 1400 m, Patm=85.6 kPa; water h₁=0.1 m, oil h₂=0.2 m (SG 0.85), mercury h₃=0.35 m (SG 13.6)
  1. Walk from the air pocket down through water and oil, then up through mercury to reach atmosphere: P₁ + ρwgh₁ + ρoilgh₂ − ρHggh₃ = Patm
  2. Rearrange for P₁: P₁ = Patm − ρwgh₁ − ρoilgh₂ + ρHggh₃
  3. Substitute (ρ in kg/m³, g=9.81, dividing by 1000 for kPa): P₁ = 85.6 + 9.81[(13600)(0.35) − (1000)(0.1) − (850)(0.2)]/1000
  4. Inside brackets: 4760 − 100 − 170 = 4490
  5. P₁ = 85.6 + 9.81(4490)/1000 = 85.6 + 44.05 = 129.65 ≈ 130 kPa
Pair ≈ 130 kPa

Pressure ↔ head conversion

Q: A gauge pressure of 350 kN/m² is measured. Express this as a head of (a) water, (b) mercury (relative density 13.6). Find the absolute pressure if Patm=101.3 kN/m².
Gauge pressure = 350 kN/m²; Patm=101.3 kN/m²
  1. (a) h = P/ρg = (350×10³)/(1000×9.81) = 35.68 m of water
  2. (b) h = P/(SG·ρw·g) = (350×10³)/(13.6×1000×9.81) = 2.62 m of mercury
  3. Absolute pressure: Pabs = Pgage + Patm = 350 + 101.3 = 451.3 kN/m²
Absolute = 451.3 kN/m²

Exam checklist

  • Always draw the manometer and label every interface before writing equations.
  • Keep units consistent (Pa vs kPa) — this is the #1 source of silly mistakes.
  • Watch the sign convention: down = +ρgh, up = −ρgh.

Core formulas

Resultant hydrostatic force: FR = ρg hc A  (hc = depth of centroid)
Center of pressure: hp = hc + Ic / (hc A)
Buoyancy: FB = ρfluid g Vdisplaced
Floating/submerged body equilibrium: Weight = Buoyancy (+ any tension)

Ic for a circle = πD⁴/64 (about its own centroid); for a rectangle = bh³/12.

Worked examples — hydrostatic force

Circular plane surface (vertical)

Q: A circular surface of diameter 2 m is vertical, with its top edge 0.5 m below the water surface. Find the resultant force and the depth of the center of pressure.
D=2 m, top edge 0.5 m below surface
  1. Centroid depth: hc = 0.5 + D/2 = 0.5 + 1 = 1.5 m
  2. Area: A = πD²/4 = π(2)²/4 = π m²
  3. Resultant force: FR = ρg hc A = 1000×9.81×1.5×π = 46,234 N = 46.2 kN
  4. Second moment of area about centroid (circle): Ic = πr⁴/4 = π(1)⁴/4 = 0.7854 m⁴
  5. Center of pressure: hp = hc + Ic/(hcA) = 1.5 + 0.7854/(1.5×π) = 1.5 + 0.167 = 1.667 m
FR=46.2 kN, hp=1.667 m

Plywood sheet blocking concrete (hinged gate)

Q: A plywood sheet 1.22 m × 2.44 m blocks wet concrete against a wall. It's hinged at the top with a stop at the bottom. Concrete depth is 0.96 m, SG=2.4. Find (a) the resultant force and its location, (b) the force on the stop.
1.22 m × 2.44 m, concrete depth 0.96 m, SG=2.4
  1. Density: ρ = SG×1000 = 2400 kg/m³
  2. Centroid depth: hc = 0.96/2 = 0.48 m
  3. Wetted area: A = 0.96 × 2.44 = 2.342 m²
  4. Resultant force: FR = ρg hc A = 2400×9.81×0.48×2.342 = 26,470 N = 26.47 kN
  5. Ic (rectangle) = b h³/12 = (2.44)(0.96)³/12 = 0.180 m⁴
  6. Center of pressure: hp = hc + Ic/(hcA) = 0.48 + 0.180/1.124 = 0.64 m
  7. Moments about the hinge (top): Fstop × 0.96 = FR × hp → Fstop = (26.47×0.64)/0.96 = 17.65 kN
FR=26.47 kN, Fstop=17.65 kN

Circular window, cruise ship

Q: A 30-cm diameter circular window on a cruise ship has its midpoint 5 m below the water surface. Seawater SG=1.025. Find the force and the pressure center.
D=30 cm, midpoint 5 m deep, seawater SG=1.025
  1. Average pressure: Pavg = ρg hc = 1025×9.81×5 = 50,276 N/m²
  2. Area: A = πD²/4 = π(0.3)²/4 = 0.0707 m²
  3. Force: FR = Pavg A = 50,276×0.0707 = 3554 N
  4. Pressure center: yp = yc + R²/(4yc) = 5 + (0.15)²/(4×5) = 5.0011 m
FR≈3550 N, yp≈5.00 m

Worked examples — buoyancy

Sphere lowered into lake

Q: A 1-m diameter spherical steel block (ρ=8050 kg/m³) is lowered into a lake by a crane. Find the cable tension when (a) suspended in air, (b) completely immersed.
D=1 m steel sphere, ρs=8050 kg/m³
  1. Volume: V = πD³/6 = π(1)³/6 = 0.5236 m³
  2. Weight: W = ρsgV = 8050×9.81×0.5236 = 41,350 N
  3. (a) In air, tension = weight: T = 41.35 kN
  4. Buoyancy: FB = ρwgV = 1000×9.81×0.5236 = 5137 N
  5. (b) Immersed: T = W − FB = 41,350 − 5137 = 36,213 N = 36.2 kN
In air: 41.35 kN  |  Immersed: 36.2 kN

Spring scale in water

Q: A body weighs 7200 N in air and 4790 N when fully submerged in water. Find its volume and density.
Weighs 7200 N in air, 4790 N in water
  1. Buoyancy = weight lost in water: FB = 7200 − 4790 = 2410 N
  2. Volume: V = FB/(ρwg) = 2410/(1000×9.81) = 0.2457 m³
  3. Mass: m = W/g = 7200/9.81 = 734 kg
  4. Density: ρ = m/V = 734/0.2457 = 2987 kg/m³
V=0.2457 m³, ρ=2987 kg/m³

Exam checklist

  • Find the centroid depth hc first — it's the anchor for everything else.
  • Center of pressure is always below the centroid (hp > hc) for a vertical/inclined surface.
  • For buoyancy problems, "weight in water" = actual weight − buoyant force.

Continuity (conservation of mass)

ṁ = ρAV (mass flow rate)  |  ρ₁A₁V₁ = ρ₂A₂V₂
Incompressible flow: A₁V₁ = A₂V₂ = Q (volume flow rate)

Tapering duct

Q: A rectangular duct is 300 mm × 450 mm carrying a flow rate of 0.42 m³/s. Find the mean velocity. If the duct tapers to 150 mm × 400 mm, find the new velocity.
Q=0.42 m³/s; duct 300×450 mm tapering to 150×400 mm
  1. Initial area: A₁ = 0.3×0.45 = 0.135 m²
  2. V₁ = Q/A₁ = 0.42/0.135 = 3.11 m/s
  3. Tapered area: A₂ = 0.15×0.4 = 0.06 m²
  4. V₂ = Q/A₂ = 0.42/0.06 = 7.0 m/s  (Q stays the same by continuity)
V₁=3.11 m/s → V₂=7.0 m/s

Pipe fork (A→B→C splits to D and E)

Q: Pipe AB has d=1.2 m, V=3 m/s. It connects to pipe BC with d=1.5 m. At C the pipe forks: CD (d=0.8 m) carries ⅓ of the flow in AB, and CE has velocity 2.5 m/s. Find (a) QAB, (b) VBC, (c) VCD, (d) dCE.
AB: d=1.2 m,V=3 m/s. CD carries ⅓ of QAB, d=0.8 m. CE at 2.5 m/s.
  1. (a) QAB = AABVAB = π(1.2)²/4 × 3 = 3.393 m³/s
  2. (b) VBC = QAB/ABC = 3.393/[π(1.5)²/4] = 1.92 m/s  (same Q flows through BC)
  3. (c) QCD = ⅓×QAB = 1.131 m³/s → VCD = 1.131/[π(0.8)²/4] = 2.25 m/s
  4. (d) QCE = QAB − QCD = 3.393 − 1.131 = 2.262 m³/s (mass balance at the junction)
  5. ACE = QCE/VCE = 2.262/2.5 = 0.9048 m² → dCE = √(4A/π) = 1.073 m
VBC=1.92, VCD=2.25 m/s, dCE=1.073 m

Bernoulli's equation

P/ρg + V²/2g + z = constant (along a streamline, no losses)

Torricelli — tank draining

Q: A large tank open to the atmosphere has a water height of 5 m above an outlet tap. Find the velocity at the outlet.
Water height 5 m above outlet, open to atmosphere
  1. Bernoulli between surface (1) and outlet (2): P₁/ρg + V₁²/2g + z₁ = P₂/ρg + V₂²/2g + z₂
  2. P₁=P₂=Patm (both open to air); V₁≈0 (large surface); take z₂=0, so z₁=5 m
  3. Equation reduces to: z₁ = V₂²/2g
  4. V₂ = √(2g z₁) = √(2×9.81×5) = 9.9 m/s
V₂ = 9.9 m/s

Tapering inclined pipe

Q: A pipe inclined at 45° converges from d₁=200 mm to d₂=100 mm over a length of 2 m. Oil (SG=0.9) flows with V₁=2 m/s. Find (a) the pressure difference between the two ends, (b) the mercury manometer reading.
45° incline, d₁=200→d₂=100 mm over 2 m, oil SG=0.9, V₁=2 m/s
  1. Continuity: V₂ = V₁(d₁/d₂)² = 2×(200/100)² = 8 m/s
  2. Elevation rise along the incline: z₂−z₁ = 2×sin45° = 1.414 m
  3. Bernoulli rearranged: P₁−P₂ = ½ρ(V₂²−V₁²) + ρg(z₂−z₁)
  4. Substitute: P₁−P₂ = 0.5×900×(64−4) + 900×9.81×1.414 = 27,000 + 12,484 = 39,484 N/m² = 39.5 kPa
  5. Manometer reading: h = (P₁−P₂)/[(ρHg−ρoil)g] = 39,484/[(13600−900)×9.81] = 0.317 m
P₁−P₂ = 39.5 kPa, manometer h = 0.317 m

Venturi meter

Q: A venturi meter has d₁=250 mm and d₂=100 mm, carrying oil (SG=0.9). A mercury manometer connected across it reads 0.63 m. Find the theoretical volume flow rate.
d₁=250 mm, d₂=100 mm, oil SG=0.9, Hg manometer reading 0.63 m
  1. Areas: A₁ = π(0.25)²/4 = 0.0491 m², A₂ = π(0.10)²/4 = 0.00785 m²
  2. Venturi formula: Q = [A₁/√((A₁/A₂)²−1)] × √(2gh(ρHg/ρoil −1))
  3. Area ratio term: (A₁/A₂)² −1 = (6.25)² −1 = 38.1 → √38.1 = 6.17
  4. Bracket term: 2×9.81×0.63×(13600/900 −1) = 12.36×14.11 = 174.4 → √174.4 = 13.21
  5. Q = (0.0491/6.17) × 13.21 = 0.00796×13.21 ≈ 0.105 m³/s
Q ≈ 0.105 m³/s

Jet height from a hose (pressure → elevation)

Q: A water main is at 400 kPa gage. A hose connected to it is held pointing straight up. Find the maximum height the jet reaches.
Main pressure 400 kPa gage, hose pointing straight up
  1. Bernoulli between hose outlet (1) and top of trajectory (2): P₁/ρg + V₁²/2g + z₁ = P₂/ρg + V₂²/2g + z₂
  2. At the outlet, V₁≈0 (just leaving), z₁=0; at the top, V₂=0 (momentarily stops), P₂=Patm
  3. Reduces to: z₂ = P1,gage/ρg
  4. Substitute: z₂ = 400,000/(1000×9.81) = 40.8 m
z₂ = 40.8 m

Exam checklist

  • Continuity first to find an unknown velocity, then Bernoulli to relate pressure/elevation.
  • Bernoulli needs two points on the same streamline — pick points where you know the most (usually a free surface: V≈0, P=Patm).
  • Don't forget elevation (z) terms when the pipe is inclined.

⭐ Priority topic: jet striking a plate

This is a momentum-equation problem: a fluid jet hits a plate and you find the force the jet exerts. Same core formula every time — the differences are (1) is the plate flat or inclined, (2) is the plate stationary or moving.

Force ON a flat plate normal to the jet:   F = ρ A (Vjet − Vplate)²
If plate is inclined at angle θ to the jet:   Fnormal = ρ A (Vjet − Vplate)² cos θ
Mass flow rate form:   F = ṁ · ΔV  (momentum change of the fluid)
Curved vane, jet deflected by angle θ:   Fx=ṁ(Vin,x−Vout,x),   Fy=ṁ(Vin,y−Vout,y),   resultant R=√(Fx²+Fy²)

Use the relative velocity (Vjet − Vplate) whenever the plate is moving. If it's stationary, Vplate=0 and the formula reduces to F=ρAV².

Worked examples

Ex 5.1 — Jet on inclined flat plate, varying angle

Q: A jet of diameter 25 mm and velocity 5 m/s strikes an inclined flat plate. Find the force normal to the plate as the angle of inclination varies (0°, 30°, 60°, 90°).
d=25 mm, V=5 m/s, stationary plate, F=ρAV²cosθ
  1. Jet area: A = πd²/4 = π(0.025)²/4 = 4.909×10⁻⁴ m²
  2. Plate stationary → use F = ρAV²cosθ
  3. Base value (θ=0°): ρAV² = 1000×4.909×10⁻⁴×5² = 12.27 N
  4. Multiply by cosθ for each angle: 30°→12.27×cos30°=10.63 N; 60°→12.27×cos60°=6.14 N; 90°→12.27×cos90°=0 N
θ=0°: 12.27 N  |  30°: 10.63 N  |  60°: 6.14 N  |  90°: 0 N

Ex 5.2 — Curved vane, jet deflected 60°

Q: A jet is deflected 60° by a curved vane. Entry velocity is 30 m/s, exit velocity is 25 m/s, mass flow rate is 0.8 kg/s. Find the resultant force on the vane.
Vin=30 m/s, Vout=25 m/s, ṁ=0.8 kg/s
  1. Set jet-in direction as x-axis; break the exit velocity into x and y components using the 60° deflection.
  2. Fx = ṁ(Vin,x − Vout,x) = 0.8(30 − 25cos60°) = 0.8(30−12.5) = 14 N
  3. Fy = ṁ(Vin,y − Vout,y) = 0.8(0 − 25sin60°) = −0.8(21.65) = −17.32 N
  4. Resultant: R = √(Fx² + Fy²) = √(196+300) = 22.3 N
  5. Direction: φ = tan⁻¹(17.32/14) = 51° to the horizontal
Resultant R = 22.3 N at 51° to horizontal

PQ 5.1 — Flat plate, stationary vs moving

Q: A jet of diameter 50 mm and velocity 18 m/s strikes a flat plate. Find the force when (a) the plate is stationary, (b) the plate moves at 6 m/s in the same direction as the jet.
d=50 mm, V=18 m/s
  1. Jet area: A = π(0.05)²/4 = 1.963×10⁻³ m²
  2. (a) Stationary: F = ρAV² = 1000×1.963×10⁻³×18² = 636 N
  3. (b) Moving at u=6 m/s same direction — use relative velocity: F = ρA(V−u)² = 1000×1.963×10⁻³×(18−6)² = 283 N
Stationary: 636 N  |  Moving: 283 N

PQ 5.2 — Inclined plate (30° to normal), stationary vs moving

Q: A jet of d=25 mm, v=5 m/s strikes a plate inclined at 30° to the normal. Find the force normal to the plate when (a) the plate is stationary, (b) the plate moves at 2 m/s.
d=25 mm, V=5 m/s
  1. Jet area: A = π(0.025)²/4 = 4.909×10⁻⁴ m²
  2. (a) Stationary: F = ρAV²cosθ = 1000×4.909×10⁻⁴×5²×cos30° = 10.63 N
  3. (b) Moving at u=2 m/s: F = ρA(V−u)²cosθ = 1000×4.909×10⁻⁴×(5−2)²×cos30° = 3.83 N
Stationary: 10.63 N  |  Moving at 2 m/s: 3.83 N

PQ 5.3 — Plate moving in same direction as jet

Q: A jet with area 3 cm² and velocity 20 m/s strikes a plate moving at 15 m/s in the same direction. Find the force required to keep the plate moving at that speed.
A=3 cm², Vjet=20 m/s, Vplate=15 m/s
  1. Convert area: A = 3 cm² = 3×10⁻⁴ m²
  2. Relative velocity = Vjet−Vplate = 20−15 = 5 m/s
  3. F = ρA(Vjet−Vplate)² = 1000×3×10⁻⁴×5² = 7.5 N
F = 7.5 N

PQ 5.4 — Stationary plate, mass-flow form

Q: Water strikes a stationary plate at 20 m/s with a mass flow rate of 10 kg/s. Find the force on the plate.
V=20 m/s, ṁ=10 kg/s
  1. When ṁ is given directly, skip finding A — just use the momentum form: F = ṁV
  2. F = ṁV = 10×20 = 200 N
F = 200 N

PQ 5.8 — Plate on a track (accelerating, not steady)

Q: A 5-cm diameter jet at V=18 m/s strikes a plate of mass 1000 kg free to move on a track, starting at rest. Find (a) the acceleration at t=0, (b) the time to reach 9 m/s, (c) the velocity at t=20 s.
d=5 cm, V=18 m/s, plate mass=1000 kg, starts at rest
  1. Jet area: A = π(0.05)²/4 = 1.963×10⁻³ m²
  2. (a) Initial force (plate at rest, u=0): F₀ = ρAV² = 1000×1.963×10⁻³×18² = 636 N → a₀ = F₀/m = 636/1000 = 0.636 m/s²
  3. As the plate speeds up, the relative velocity (V−u) shrinks, so the force — and acceleration — keep dropping. This means m(du/dt) = ρA(V−u)², which must be integrated over time rather than solved with a single formula.
  4. (b) Integrating gives: t = (m/ρA)×[1/(V−u) − 1/V]. For u=9 m/s: t = (1000/1.963)×(1/9 − 1/18) = 28.3 s
  5. (c) Rearranging the same relation for t=20 s: 1/(18−u) = 1/18 + ρAt/m = 0.0556 + 0.0393 = 0.0949 → 18−u = 10.54 → u = 7.46 m/s
a₀=0.636 m/s²  |  t(9 m/s)=28.3 s  |  V(20 s)=7.46 m/s

Bonus — same family (jet momentum, not literally a "plate")

Good extra practice if time allows — same F=ṁΔV logic, different geometry.

PQ 5.7 — Cart with jet (braking force + power)

Q: A water jet at 15 m/s strikes a cart moving at 5 m/s in the same direction, with mass flow rate 25 kg/s. Find the braking force and the power.
Jet 15 m/s, cart moving 5 m/s same direction, ṁ=25 kg/s
  1. Relative velocity: Vrel = Vjet − Vcart = 15−5 = 10 m/s
  2. Force: F = ṁVrel = 25×10 = 250 N
  3. Power delivered to the cart: P = F × Vcart = 250×5 = 1250 W = 1.25 kW
F = 250 N, P = 1.25 kW

Exam checklist for jet-on-plate

  • Step 1: find the jet area A = πd²/4.
  • Step 2: decide if the plate is stationary (use V) or moving (use Vjet−Vplate).
  • Step 3: is it a flat plate (F=ρAV²) or inclined/curved (add cosθ, or split into x/y components)?
  • Direction of force = opposite to the direction the fluid's momentum changed — always sanity-check the sign.
  • Units: keep ρ in kg/m³, A in m², V in m/s → force comes out directly in Newtons.
