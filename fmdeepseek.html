Manometer & Pressure

Pressure, head, U-tube manometers

Formulas

FormulaMeaning
P = ρghPressure at depth h
Pgauge = Pabs − PatmGauge pressure
Pabs = Patm + ρghAbsolute pressure
SG = ρ / ρwaterSpecific gravity
h = P / (ρg)Pressure head

How to Apply — The Manometer Walk

Start at one end. Walk along the tube to the other end.
Going DOWN in a fluid → add ρgh.
Going UP in a fluid → subtract ρgh.

Key rule: Pressure is the same at the same level in the same continuous fluid.

High-Chance Exam Questions

Q1Simple U-Tube

A U-tube manometer measures pressure in a water pipe. The mercury level differs by 0.3 m. Find the gauge pressure. (ρHg = 13600 kg/m³)

Solution
P = ρgh
P = 13600 × 9.81 × 0.3
P = 40,025 Pa ≈ 40 kPa
Q2Multi-Fluid Tank

An open tank has 2 m oil (SG = 0.8) on top of 3 m water. Find the gauge pressure at the bottom.

Solution
P = ρ_oil·g·h_oil + ρ_water·g·h_water
P = (0.8×1000)(9.81)(2) + (1000)(9.81)(3)
P = 15,696 + 29,430
P = 45,126 Pa ≈ 45.1 kPa
Q3Differential Manometer

A differential manometer connects two pipes A and B. Fluid is water, manometer fluid is mercury, deflection = 0.25 m. Find PA − PB.

Solution
P_A − P_B = (ρ_Hg − ρ_water)gh
= (13600 − 1000)(9.81)(0.25)
= 30,903 Pa ≈ 30.9 kPa

Hydrostatic Force & Buoyancy

Forces on submerged surfaces, floating & sinking

Formulas

FormulaMeaning
FR = ρghCAForce on submerged surface
hCDepth to centroid (middle)
yP = yC + IC/(yCA)Center of pressure
FB = ρfluidgVdispBuoyancy force
W = ρobjgVobjWeight of object

Centroid Depths (hC) — Quick Reference

ShapehC from top
Rectangleh/2
Circleh/2 (center)
Triangle2h/3 from top
Remember: Center of pressure (yP) is always below the centroid.

How to Apply — Buoyancy

Floating: Weight = Buoyancy → ρobjVtotal = ρfluidVsub
Suspended: Weight = Buoyancy (fully submerged)
Sinking: Weight > Buoyancy → Tension = W − FB

High-Chance Exam Questions

Q1Vertical Rectangular Gate

A rectangular gate 2 m wide and 3 m high is vertical. The top is 1 m below the water surface. Find the force.

Solution
h_C = 1 + 3/2 = 2.5 m
A = 2 × 3 = 6 m²
F = ρgh_C·A
F = 1000 × 9.81 × 2.5 × 6
F = 147,150 N = 147.15 kN
Q2Floating Wooden Block

A wooden block (ρ = 600 kg/m³) floats in water. Volume = 0.5 m³. Find the volume submerged.

Solution
Weight = Buoyancy
ρ_wood × V × g = ρ_water × V_sub × g
600 × 0.5 = 1000 × V_sub
V_sub = 300 / 1000 = 0.3 m³
Q3Submerged Sphere — Tension

A 1 m diameter steel sphere (ρ = 8050 kg/m³) is submerged in water. Find the tension in the rope.

Solution
V = (4/3)πr³ = (4/3)π(0.5)³ = 0.5236 m³

Weight = 8050 × 9.81 × 0.5236 = 41,350 N
Buoyancy = 1000 × 9.81 × 0.5236 = 5,136 N

Tension = Weight − Buoyancy
Tension = 41,350 − 5,136 = 36,214 N

Mass Continuity & Bernoulli

Flow rate, conservation of mass and energy

Formulas

FormulaMeaning
Q = AVFlow rate
A₁V₁ = A₂V₂Continuity (incompressible)
ρ₁A₁V₁ = ρ₂A₂V₂Continuity (compressible)
P/ρg + V²/2g + z = constBernoulli equation
V = √(2gh)Torricelli (tank draining)

How to Apply — Bernoulli in 4 Steps

1. Write Bernoulli at point 1 and point 2
2. Cancel zero terms (large tank → V₁ = 0; open to air → P = Patm)
3. Rearrange for the unknown
4. Plug in numbers

Physical meaning: Higher velocity → lower pressure. Higher elevation → lower pressure.

High-Chance Exam Questions

Q1Continuity — Pipe Narrows

Water flows through a 200 mm pipe at 2 m/s. It narrows to 100 mm. Find the velocity at the narrow section.

Solution
A₁V₁ = A₂V₂

(π×0.2²/4)(2) = (π×0.1²/4)V₂
0.0314 × 2 = 0.00785 × V₂
V₂ = 8 m/s
Q2Torricelli — Tank Draining

A tank has 5 m water depth. A hole at the bottom. Find the exit velocity.

Solution
V = √(2gh)
V = √(2 × 9.81 × 5)
V = √98.1
V = 9.9 m/s
Q3Bernoulli — Horizontal Pipe

Water flows through a horizontal pipe. At point 1: P = 200 kPa, V = 2 m/s. At point 2: V = 6 m/s. Find P₂.

Solution
P₁/ρg + V₁²/2g = P₂/ρg + V₂²/2g

200000/9810 + 2²/19.62 = P₂/9810 + 6²/19.62
20.39 + 0.204 = P₂/9810 + 1.835

P₂/9810 = 18.76
P₂ = 184,000 Pa = 184 kPa

Momentum — Jet Striking Plate

Force from a jet of fluid hitting a surface

Formulas

FormulaMeaning
F = ρAV²Stationary plate (perpendicular)
F = ρA(V−u)²Moving plate
F = ρAV²cosθInclined plate (stationary)
F = ρA(V−u)²cosθInclined plate (moving)
ṁ = ρAVMass flow rate

How to Apply

Stationary plate: Force = ρAV²
Moving plate: Use relative velocity (V − u)
Inclined plate: Multiply by cosθ (for normal force)
Curved vane: Split into x and y components

Watch out: If the plate moves in the same direction as the jet, the relative velocity is (V − u). If it moves toward the jet, it's (V + u).

High-Chance Exam Questions

Q1Stationary Flat Plate

A 50 mm diameter water jet at 18 m/s strikes a stationary flat plate perpendicularly. Find the force.

Solution
A = π(0.05)²/4 = 0.001963 m²

F = ρAV²
F = 1000 × 0.001963 × 18²
F = 1000 × 0.001963 × 324
F = 636 N
Q2Moving Plate

Same jet (50 mm, 18 m/s) strikes a plate moving at 6 m/s in the same direction. Find the force.

Solution
F = ρA(V − u)²
F = 1000 × 0.001963 × (18 − 6)²
F = 1000 × 0.001963 × 144
F = 283 N
Q3Inclined Plate

A 25 mm jet at 5 m/s strikes a plate inclined at 30° to the jet. Find the normal force (stationary).

Solution
A = π(0.025)²/4 = 0.000491 m²

F = ρAV²cosθ
F = 1000 × 0.000491 × 5² × cos30°
F = 1000 × 0.000491 × 25 × 0.866
F = 10.6 N

Exam Tips

Easy marks you should not lose

1. Always write units — they give marks for correct units.
2. ρwater = 1000 kg/m³, g = 9.81 m/s² — memorize these.
3. Manometer: go down → add ρgh, go up → subtract ρgh.
4. Buoyancy: floating means Weight = Buoyancy.
5. Jets: if the plate moves, use relative velocity (V − u).
6. Draw a simple diagram before solving — it helps you see what's going on.
7. Check if flow is incompressible → use A₁V₁ = A₂V₂.
8. For Bernoulli: cancel zero terms first, then rearrange.

Final word: You've got this. Focus on the formulas, do the practice questions, and stay calm. 2 hours is enough if you follow the plan. 💪
