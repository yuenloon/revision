📘 Chapter 1 – Fluid Properties

Example 1: Density of air in a room

Problem: Determine the density, specific gravity, and mass of air in a room 4 m × 5 m × 6 m at 100 kPa and 25°C. R = 0.287 kPa·m³/kg·K.

ρ = P / (R T) = 100 / (0.287 × 298.15) = 1.17 kg/m³
SG = ρ / ρwater = 1.17 / 1000 = 0.00117
V = 4 × 5 × 6 = 120 m³ · m = ρV = 1.17 × 120 = 140 kg

Answer: ρ = 1.17 kg/m³, SG = 0.00117, m = 140 kg

Example 2: Minimum pressure to avoid cavitation

Problem: Water distribution system at 30°C. Determine minimum pressure to avoid cavitation. Psat at 30°C = 4.25 kPa.

Pmin = Psat@30°C = 4.25 kPa

Answer: Pressure must be maintained above 4.25 kPa.

Note: Vapour pressure increases with temperature.

📗 Chapter 2 – Pressure and Head

Example 2.1: Ocean depth pressure

Problem: Neglecting surface pressure and compressibility, what is the pressure at 4600 m below ocean surface? γ = 10.05 kN/m³.

P = γ h = 10.05 × 4600 = 46,230 kN/m²

Answer: 46,230 kN/m² = 46.23 MPa

Slide says 46,700 kN/m² — that is a calculation error. Correct is 46,230 kN/m².
Example 2.2: Manometer pressure

Problem: Manometer measures tank pressure. Fluid SG = 0.85, column height = 55 cm, Patm = 96 kPa. Determine absolute pressure.

ρ = SG × ρH2O = 0.85 × 1000 = 850 kg/m³
Pabs = Patm + ρgh = 96 + (850)(9.81)(0.55)/1000
Pabs = 96 + 4.586 = 100.6 kPa
Pgage = 100.6 – 96 = 4.6 kPa

Answer: Absolute = 100.6 kPa, Gauge = 4.6 kPa

Example 2.3: Multifluid manometer

Problem: Tank at 1400 m altitude (Patm = 85.6 kPa). h₁ = 0.1 m (water), h₂ = 0.2 m (oil, SG = 0.85), h₃ = 0.35 m (mercury, SG = 13.6). Determine air pressure.

P₁ = Patm – ρwgh₁ – ρoilgh₂ + ρHggh₃
P₁ = 85.6 + 9.81[(13600)(0.35) – (1000)(0.1) – (850)(0.2)]/1000
P₁ = 85.6 + 9.81(4760 – 100 – 170)/1000 = 85.6 + 44.05 = 129.65 kPa

Answer: Pair ≈ 130 kPa

Practice Q2.1: Diver pressure

Problem: Diver descends 30 m. Density of seawater = 1025 kg/m³. Find increase in pressure.

ΔP = ρgΔh = 1025 × 9.81 × 30 = 301,700 Pa = 301.7 kPa

Answer: Increase = 301.7 kPa

Practice Q2.2: Pressure and head

Problem: Gauge pressure = 350 kN/m². Express as head of (a) water, (b) mercury (RD = 13.6). Find absolute pressure if Patm = 101.3 kN/m².

(a) hwater = P/(ρg) = 350,000/(1000 × 9.81) = 35.68 m
(b) hHg = 350,000/(13.6 × 1000 × 9.81) = 2.62 m
Pabs = 350 + 101.3 = 451.3 kN/m²

Answer: (a) 35.68 m water, (b) 2.62 m Hg, Absolute = 451.3 kN/m²

Practice Q2.3: Hydraulic system

Problem: Piston A₁ = 21 cm², F₁ = 38 N. Second piston A₂ = 100 cm². Find F₂.

F₂ = F₁ × (A₂/A₁) = 38 × (100/21) = 181 N

Answer: F₂ = 181 N

Practice Q2.4: Dentist's chair

Problem: F₂ = 2269 N, A₁ = 88.12 cm², A₂ = 1221 cm². Find F₁.

F₁ = F₂ × A₁ / A₂ = 2269 × 88.12 / 1221 = 163.8 N

Answer: F₁ = 163.8 N

Problem 3‑43: Hydraulic lift

Problem: 500 kg load on hydraulic lift. Oil density = 780 kg/m³. Determine height h in thin tube (d = 1 cm) to raise weight. (Piston diameter D = 1.2 m assumed)

Pgage = W/A = mg/(πD²/4) = 500 × 9.81 / (π × 1.2²/4) = 4337 Pa
h = Pgage/(ρg) = 4340 / (780 × 9.81) = 0.567 m

Answer: h = 0.567 m = 56.7 cm

Problem 3‑50: Air pressure change in manometer

Problem: Change of 0.7 kPa in air pressure causes brine‑mercury interface to drop by 5 mm. Brine SG = 1.1, Mercury SG = 13.56. Find A₂/A₁.

ΔP = ρHggΔhHg + ρbrinegΔhbrine
Using continuity A₁Δh₁ = A₂Δh₂, solving gives A₂/A₁ ≈ 0.050

Answer: A₂/A₁ ≈ 0.05

📙 Chapter 3 – Fluid Statics

Example 3.1: Circular plane surface

Problem: Circular surface diameter = 2 m, vertical, top edge 0.5 m below water surface. Find force and depth of centre of pressure.

hc = 0.5 + 2/2 = 1.5 m · A = πD²/4 = π m²
FR = ρghcA = 1000 × 9.81 × 1.5 × π = 46,234 N = 46.2 kN
hp = hc + Ic/(hcA) = 1.5 + (π×1⁴/4)/(1.5×π) = 1.667 m

Answer: FR = 46.2 kN, hp = 1.667 m

Example 3.2: Plywood sheet blocking concrete

Problem: Plywood 1.22 m × 2.44 m blocks concrete. Hinged at top, stop at bottom. Concrete depth = 0.96 m, SG = 2.4. Find (a) resultant force and location, (b) force on stop.

ρ = 2.4 × 1000 = 2400 kg/m³ · hc = 0.48 m · A = 0.96 × 2.44 = 2.342 m²
FR = 2400 × 9.81 × 0.48 × 2.342 = 26,470 N = 26.47 kN
hp = 0.48 + (2.44×0.96³/12)/(0.48×2.342) = 0.64 m
Fstop = FR × hp/0.96 = 26.47 × 0.64/0.96 = 17.65 kN

Answer: FR = 26.47 kN at hp = 0.64 m; Fstop = 17.65 kN

Example 3.3: Circular window on cruise ship

Problem: 30‑cm diameter circular window, midpoint 5 m below water surface. Seawater SG = 1.025. Find force and pressure centre.

Pavg = ρghc = 1025 × 9.81 × 5 = 50,276 N/m²
FR = PavgA = 50,276 × π(0.3)²/4 = 3554 N ≈ 3550 N
yp = 5 + (0.15)²/(4×5) = 5.0011 m ≈ 5.00 m

Answer: FR = 3550 N, yp = 5.00 m

Example 3.4: Vertical trapezoidal gate

Problem: Find resultant force and centre of pressure. (Trapezoid: top width 3 m, bottom width 1 m, height 2 m, top edge at 5 m depth.)

hc = 5 + [2(2×1 + 3)]/[3(1 + 3)] = 5 + 10/12 = 5.83 m
A = 2(3 + 1)/2 = 4 m²
FR = 1000 × 9.81 × 5.83 × 4 = 228,900 N = 228.9 kN
Io = 2³(1² + 4×1×3 + 3²)/[36(1+3)] = 1.22 m⁴
hp = 5.83 + 1.22/(5.83×4) = 5.88 m

Answer: FR = 228.9 kN, hp = 5.88 m

Example 3.5: Reservoir wall

Problem: 25‑m deep reservoir, 150‑m wide wall (equilateral triangle). Patm = 100 kPa. Find total force (hydrostatic + atmospheric).

hc = 12.5 m · base = 25/tan60° = 28.87 m · A = 150 × 28.87 = 4330.5 m²
FR = (Patm + ρghc)A = (100,000 + 1000×9.81×12.5) × 4330.5
FR = 222,625 × 4330.5 = 9.64 × 10⁸ N

Answer: FR = 9.64 × 10⁸ N

Question 1: Crane lowering sphere

Problem: 1‑m diameter spherical steel block (ρ = 8050 kg/m³) lowered into lake. Find tension when (a) suspended in air, (b) completely immersed.

V = πD³/6 = 0.5236 m³ · W = 8050 × 9.81 × 0.5236 = 41,350 N
(a) Tair = W = 41.35 kN
(b) FB = 1000 × 9.81 × 0.5236 = 5137 N · T = 41,350 – 5137 = 36,213 N

Answer: (a) 41.35 kN, (b) 36.2 kN

Question 2: Spring scale

Problem: Body weighs 7200 N in air, 4790 N in water. Find volume and density.

FB = 7200 – 4790 = 2410 N · V = FB/(ρwg) = 2410/(1000×9.81) = 0.2457 m³
ρ = (W/g)/V = (7200/9.81)/0.2457 = 2987 kg/m³

Answer: V = 0.2457 m³, ρ = 2987 kg/m³

📒 Chapter 4 – Conservation of Mass

Example 4.1: Garden hose

Problem: Fill 10‑gal bucket in 50 s. Hose inner diameter = 2 cm, nozzle exit diameter = 0.8 cm. Find (a) volume and mass flow rates, (b) average velocity at nozzle exit.

(a) Q = 10 gal / 50 s × 3.7854 L/gal = 0.757 L/s · ṁ = 0.757 kg/s
(b) Ae = π(0.4 cm)² = 0.5027 cm² = 5.027×10⁻⁵ m²
Ve = Q/Ae = 0.757×10⁻³ / 5.027×10⁻⁵ = 15.1 m/s

Answer: (a) 0.757 L/s, 0.757 kg/s; (b) 15.1 m/s

Practice Q4.1: Air duct

Problem: Rectangular duct 300 mm × 450 mm, flow rate = 0.42 m³/s. Find mean velocity. If duct tapers to 150 mm × 400 mm, find new velocity.

A₁ = 0.3 × 0.45 = 0.135 m² · V₁ = 0.42/0.135 = 3.11 m/s
A₂ = 0.15 × 0.4 = 0.06 m² · V₂ = 0.42/0.06 = 7.0 m/s

Answer: V₁ = 3.11 m/s, V₂ = 7.0 m/s

Practice Q4.2: Air compressor

Problem: Air enters compressor: ρ = 1.2 kg/m³, V = 5 m/s, A = 20 cm². (a) Find mass flow rate. (b) Air leaves through 25 mm diameter pipe at 4 m/s. Find density.

(a) ṁ = ρAV = 1.2 × 20×10⁻⁴ × 5 = 0.012 kg/s
(b) A₂ = π(0.025)²/4 = 4.909×10⁻⁴ m² · ρ₂ = ṁ/(A₂V₂) = 0.012/(4.909×10⁻⁴ × 4) = 6.11 kg/m³

Answer: (a) 0.012 kg/s, (b) 6.11 kg/m³

Practice Q4.3: Pipe fork

Problem: Pipe AB: d = 1.2 m, V = 3 m/s. Pipe BC: d = 1.5 m. At C, forks: CD (d = 0.8 m) carries 1/3 of flow in AB. CE velocity = 2.5 m/s. Find (a) QAB, (b) VBC, (c) VCD, (d) dCE.

(a) QAB = π(1.2)²/4 × 3 = 3.393 m³/s
(b) VBC = 3.393 / (π(1.5)²/4) = 1.92 m/s
(c) QCD = 3.393/3 = 1.131 m³/s · VCD = 1.131/(π(0.8)²/4) = 2.25 m/s
(d) QCE = 3.393 – 1.131 = 2.262 m³/s · ACE = 2.262/2.5 = 0.9048 m² · dCE = 1.073 m

Answer: (a) 3.393 m³/s, (b) 1.92 m/s, (c) 2.25 m/s, (d) 1.073 m

Practice Q4.4: Closed tank mixing

Problem: Inlet A: d = 150 mm, Q = 56 dm³/s, SG = 0.93. Inlet B: d = 100 mm, Q = 30 dm³/s, SG = 0.87. Outlet C: d = 175 mm. Find mass flow rate, velocity, and SG of mixture.

ṁA = 0.93 × 1000 × 56×10⁻³ = 52.08 kg/s · ṁB = 0.87 × 1000 × 30×10⁻³ = 26.1 kg/s
ṁC = 78.18 kg/s · QC = 86 dm³/s = 0.086 m³/s
VC = 0.086/(π(0.175)²/4) = 3.57 m/s · ρC = 78.18/0.086 = 909 kg/m³ · SG = 0.909

Answer: ṁ = 78.18 kg/s, V = 3.57 m/s, SG = 0.909

Practice Q4.5: Computer fan

Problem: Fan flow rate = 0.34 m³/min, elevation 3400 m (ρ = 0.7 kg/m³). Find mass flow rate. If average velocity ≤ 110 m/min, find casing diameter.

ṁ = 0.7 × (0.34/60) = 0.00397 kg/s
V = 110 m/min = 1.833 m/s · A = (0.34/60)/1.833 = 0.00309 m²
D = √(4A/π) = √(4×0.00309/π) = 0.0627 m = 6.27 cm

Answer: ṁ = 0.00397 kg/s, D = 6.27 cm

Practice Q4.6: Hair dryer

Problem: Air density at inlet = 1.20 kg/m³, at exit = 1.05 kg/m³. Find percent increase in velocity.

ρ₁V₁ = ρ₂V₂ · V₂/V₁ = ρ₁/ρ₂ = 1.20/1.05 = 1.143
% increase = (1.143 – 1) × 100% = 14.3%

Answer: 14.3% increase

📒 Chapter 4 Part 2 – Energy Equations

Example 4.1: Tank with outlet

Problem: Large tank open to atmosphere, water height 5 m from outlet tap. Find velocity at outlet.

z₁ = V₂²/(2g) → V₂ = √(2gz₁) = √(2×9.81×5) = 9.9 m/s

Answer: V₂ = 9.9 m/s (Torricelli equation)

Example 4.2: Hose jet height

Problem: Water main at 400 kPa gage. Hose held upward. Find maximum height of jet.

z₂ = P1,gage/(ρg) = 400,000/(1000×9.81) = 40.8 m

Answer: z₂ = 40.8 m

Example 4.4: Tapering pipe

Problem: Pipe inclined 45°, converges from d₁ = 200 mm to d₂ = 100 mm over 2 m. Oil SG = 0.9, V₁ = 2 m/s. Find (a) pressure difference, (b) manometer reading.

V₂ = V₁(d₁/d₂)² = 2 × (200/100)² = 8 m/s
z₂ – z₁ = 2 sin45° = 1.414 m
P₁ – P₂ = ½ρ(V₂² – V₁²) + ρg(z₂ – z₁)
= 0.5×900(64 – 4) + 900×9.81×1.414 = 27,000 + 12,484 = 39,484 N/m²
(b) h = (P₁ – P₂)/[(ρHg – ρoil)g] = 39,484/((13600–900)×9.81) = 0.317 m

Answer: P₁ – P₂ = 39.5 kPa, h = 0.317 m

Example 4.5: Venturi meter

Problem: Venturi meter d₁ = 250 mm, d₂ = 100 mm. Oil SG = 0.9. Mercury manometer reading = 0.63 m. Find theoretical volume flow rate.

A₁ = π(0.25)²/4 = 0.0491 m² · A₂ = π(0.10)²/4 = 0.00785 m²
Q = (A₁/√((A₁/A₂)²–1)) × √(2gh(ρman/ρ – 1))
Q = (0.0491/√(39.1–1)) × √(2×9.81×0.63×(13600/900 – 1))
Q = (0.0491/6.17) × √(12.36 × 14.11) = 0.00796 × 13.21 = 0.105 m³/s

Answer: Q ≈ 0.105 m³/s

Slide says 0.0977 m³/s — likely used slightly different values.

📕 Chapter 5 – Momentum Equation

Example 5.1: Jet on inclined flat plate

Problem: Jet diameter = 25 mm, velocity = 5 m/s, strikes inclined flat plate. Find force normal to plate as inclination varies.

F = ρAv²cosθ (stationary) · A = π(0.025)²/4 = 4.909×10⁻⁴ m²
ρAv² = 1000 × 4.909×10⁻⁴ × 25 = 12.27 N
θ = 0°: 12.27 N · θ = 30°: 10.63 N · θ = 60°: 6.14 N · θ = 90°: 0 N

Answer: Force varies as 12.27cosθ N

Example 5.2: Curved vane

Problem: Jet deflected 60° by curved vane. Entry velocity = 30 m/s, exit = 25 m/s. Mass flow = 0.8 kg/s. Find resultant force.

Fx = 0.8(30 – 25cos60°) = 0.8(30 – 12.5) = 14 N
Fy = –0.8(25sin60°) = –17.32 N
R = √(14² + 17.32²) = 22.3 N · φ = tan⁻¹(17.32/14) = 51°

Answer: R = 22.3 N at 51° to the horizontal

Practice Q5.1: Flat plate (stationary & moving)

Problem: Jet diameter = 50 mm, velocity = 18 m/s. Find force when (a) stationary, (b) moving at 6 m/s in same direction.

A = π(0.05)²/4 = 1.963×10⁻³ m²
(a) F = 1000 × 1.963×10⁻³ × 18² = 636 N
(b) F = 1000 × 1.963×10⁻³ × (18–6)² = 283 N

Answer: (a) 636 N, (b) 283 N

Practice Q5.2: Inclined plate (moving)

Problem: d = 25 mm, v = 5 m/s, angle = 30° to normal. Find force normal to plate (a) stationary, (b) moving at 2 m/s.

A = 4.909×10⁻⁴ m²
(a) F = 1000 × 4.909×10⁻⁴ × 25 × cos30° = 10.63 N
(b) F = 1000 × 4.909×10⁻⁴ × (5–2)² × cos30° = 3.83 N

Answer: (a) 10.63 N, (b) 3.83 N

Practice Q5.3: Moving plate

Problem: Jet area = 3 cm², Vj = 20 m/s, plate moves at Vc = 15 m/s. Find force to keep plate moving.

F = 1000 × 3×10⁻⁴ × (20–15)² = 7.5 N

Answer: F = 7.5 N

Practice Q5.4: Stationary plate

Problem: Water strikes stationary plate at 20 m/s, mass flow = 10 kg/s. Find force.

F = ṁV = 10 × 20 = 200 N

Answer: F = 200 N

Practice Q5.6: Reducing elbow

Problem: Water flow = 30 kg/s, deflected 45° upward. Inlet area = 150 cm², exit area = 25 cm². Elevation difference = 40 cm. Mass of elbow + water = 50 kg. Find anchoring force.

V₁ = 30/(1000×0.015) = 2 m/s · V₂ = 30/(1000×0.0025) = 12 m/s

FRx + P₁A₁ = ṁ(V₂cos45° – V₁) → FRx = 194.5 – 1109 = –914.5 N
FRz – W = ṁV₂sin45° → FRz = 254.5 + 490.5 = 745 N

Practice Q5.7: Cart with brakes

Problem: Water jet velocity = 15 m/s, cart moves at 5 m/s, mass flow = 25 kg/s. Find braking force and power.

Vrel = 15 – 5 = 10 m/s · F = ṁVrel = 25 × 10 = 250 N
P = F × Vc = 250 × 5 = 1250 W = 1.25 kW

Answer: F = 250 N, P = 1.25 kW

Practice Q5.8: Plate on track (differential equation)

Problem: 5‑cm diameter jet, V = 18 m/s, plate mass = 1000 kg. Find (a) acceleration at t = 0, (b) time to reach 9 m/s, (c) velocity at 20 s.

F₀ = 1000 × π(0.05)²/4 × 18² = 636 N · a₀ = 636/1000 = 0.636 m/s²
(b) t = (m/ρA)(1/(V–u) – 1/V) = 28.3 s
(c) 1/(18–u) = 1/18 + ρAt/m → u = 7.46 m/s

Answer: (a) 0.636 m/s², (b) 28.3 s, (c) 7.46 m/s

📔 Chapter 6 Part 1 – Laminar and Turbulent Flow

Example 6.1: Maximum velocity for laminar flow

Problem: Water density = 1000 kg/m³, pipe diameter = 0.5 m, viscosity = 0.55×10⁻³ Ns/m². Find maximum velocity for laminar flow.

Re = ρVD/μ = 2000 → V = 2000 × 0.55×10⁻³ / (1000 × 0.5) = 0.0022 m/s

Answer: V = 0.0022 m/s

Example 6.2: Oil flow type

Problem: Oil viscosity = 0.05 kg/m·s, density = 860 kg/m³, pipe diameter = 0.1 m, velocity = 0.6 m/s. Determine flow type.

Re = 860 × 0.6 × 0.1 / 0.05 = 1032

Answer: Laminar flow (Re < 2300)

Practice Q2: Water and oil flow type

Problem: Water flows through 25 mm pipe at 6 m/s. μ = 1.30×10⁻³ kg/m·s, ρ = 1000 kg/m³. Determine flow type. If oil (SG = 0.9, μ = 9.6×10⁻² kg/m·s) is pumped, what type?

Water: Re = 1000 × 6 × 0.025 / 1.30×10⁻³ = 115,385 → Turbulent
Oil: Re = 900 × 6 × 0.025 / 9.6×10⁻² = 1406 → Laminar

Answer: Water: turbulent, Oil: laminar

Practice Q6.2: Oil flow rate (horizontal, uphill, downhill)

Problem: Oil at 20°C (ρ = 888 kg/m³, μ = 0.800 kg/m·s) flows through 5‑cm diameter, 40‑m long pipe. P₁ = 745 kPa, P₂ = 97 kPa. Find flow rate for (a) horizontal, (b) 15° upward, (c) 15° downward.

ΔP = 648 kPa · (a) Q = ΔP πD⁴/(128μL) = 0.00311 m³/s
(b) Q = (ΔP – ρgLsinθ)πD⁴/(128μL) = 0.00267 m³/s
(c) Q = (ΔP + ρgLsinθ)πD⁴/(128μL) = 0.00354 m³/s
Verification: Re = 100 < 2300 → laminar confirmed

Answer: (a) 0.00311, (b) 0.00267, (c) 0.00354 m³/s; Re = 100 (laminar)

Practice Q6.3: Oil discharge from small pipe

Problem: Oil density = 850 kg/m³, kinematic viscosity = 0.00062 m²/s, pipe diameter = 5 mm, length = 40 m, height = 3 m. Find flow rate, velocity, verify laminar.

μ = ρν = 850 × 0.00062 = 0.527 kg/m·s · P = ρgh = 25,016 Pa
Q = 25,020 × π × (0.005)⁴ / (128×0.527×40) = 1.821×10⁻⁸ m³/s
V = 9.27×10⁻⁴ m/s · Re = 850 × 9.27×10⁻⁴ × 0.005 / 0.527 = 0.0075

Answer: Q = 1.82×10⁻⁸ m³/s, V = 9.27×10⁻⁴ m/s, Re = 0.0075 (laminar)

Practice Q6.4: Glycerine flow

Problem: Glycerine viscosity = 0.9 N·s/m², density = 1260 kg/m³, pipe length = 6.5 m, diameter = 0.01 m, flow rate = 1.8 L/min. Find Re, pressure loss, max flow rate for laminar.

u = Q/A = (1.8×10⁻³/60)/(π(0.01)²/4) = 0.382 m/s
Re = 1260 × 0.382 × 0.01 / 0.9 = 5.35
Δp = 128μLQ/(πD⁴) = 715×10³ N/m² = 715 kPa
Qmax = Re μ πD³/(4ρ) = 2300×0.9×π×0.001/(4×1260) = 1.29×10⁻³ m³/s

Answer: Re = 5.35 (laminar), Δp = 715 kPa, Qmax = 1.29×10⁻³ m³/s

Slide says Re = 0.535 — that is a calculation error. Correct is 5.35.

📓 Chapter 6 Part 2 – Flow in Pipes

Practice Q1: Head loss due to friction

Problem: Pipe diameter = 500 mm, length = 1.5 km, velocity = 1 m/s, f = 0.02. Find head loss.

hf = f(L/D)(V²/2g) = 0.02 × (1500/0.5) × (1²/19.62) = 3.06 m

Answer: hf = 3.06 m

Practice Q2: Pipe diameter from head loss

Problem: Pipe length = 1500 m, velocity = 0.8 m/s, head loss = 8.7 m, f = 0.04. Find diameter.

Practice Q1: Valve closure (slow vs rapid)

Problem: Pipe diameter = 50 cm, length = 100 m, discharge = 0.5 m³/s. Sonic velocity = 1430 m/s. Find pressure rise for (i) closure in 1 s, (ii) instantaneous closure.

(ii) Rapid (t < tc): Δp = ρCV = 3.64×10⁶ Pa = 3.64 MPa

Practice Q5: Reservoir discharge (two closure times)

Problem: Velocity = 2 m/s, diameter = 100 cm, length = 2500 m, thickness = 2.5 cm, Kwater = 2 GPa, Esteel = 200 GPa, ρ = 1000 kg/m³. Find water hammer pressure for (a) t = 1 s, (b) t = 8 s.

Example 1: Car drag coefficient

Problem: Car at 1 atm, 25°C, 90 km/h. Height = 1.40 m, width = 1.65 m. Drag force = 350 N. Find drag coefficient.

ρ = 1.164 kg/m³ · V = 90/3.6 = 25 m/s · A = 1.40 × 1.65 = 2.31 m²
CD = 2FD/(ρAV²) = 2×350/(1.164×2.31×25²) = 0.42

Answer: CD = 0.42

Example 2: Flat plate drag (oil)

Problem: Engine oil at 40°C flows over 5‑m long flat plate at 2 m/s. Find drag force per unit width. ρ = 876 kg/m³, ν = 2.485×10⁻⁴ m²/s.

ReL = VL/ν = 2×5/2.485×10⁻⁴ = 40,241 → laminar
Cf = 1.33/√ReL = 1.33/√40241 = 0.00662
FD = CfA(ρV²/2) = 0.00662 × 5 × (876×4/2) = 58.0 N

Answer: FD = 58.0 N per unit width
