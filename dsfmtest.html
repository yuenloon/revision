Ch 2 Manometer

Pressure measurement using U-tube and differential manometers. Focus on tracing pressure from one point to another through different fluids.

📐 Key Formulas – Manometer

P2 = P1   (same level, same continuous fluid)
P = Patm + ρgh   (pressure increases with depth)
Pgage = ρgh
Pabs = Pgage + Patm
ρ = SG × ρwater   (ρwater = 1000 kg/m³)
  • Going down → add ρgh
  • Going up → subtract ρgh
  • Ignore air columns (ρair ≈ 0)
Example 2.2 – Manometer in a Tank
Image: U-tube manometer connected to a tank. Fluid SG = 0.85, column height h = 55 cm. Atmospheric pressure = 96 kPa.
A manometer is used to measure the pressure in a tank. The fluid used has a specific gravity of 0.85, and the manometer column height is 55 cm. If the local atmospheric pressure is 96 kPa, determine the absolute pressure within the tank.
Given:
  • SG = 0.85
  • h = 55 cm = 0.55 m
  • Patm = 96 kPa
  • ρwater = 1000 kg/m³
  • g = 9.81 m/s²
Formulas:
ρ = SG × ρwater
P = Patm + ρgh
Step-by-step:
1ρ = 0.85 × 1000 = 850 kg/m³
2P = 96 kPa + (850)(9.81)(0.55)
3P = 96,000 + 4,586.2 = 100,586 Pa ≈ 100.6 kPa
Absolute pressure = 100.6 kPa
Quiz Double-U Manometer – Gasoline Line
Image: Double-U manometer. Pressure gage reads 370 kPa. Fluids: Water (45 cm), Oil SG = 0.79 (50 cm), Mercury SG = 13.6 (22 cm), Gasoline SG = 0.70 (10 cm).
A gasoline line is connected to a pressure gage through a double-U manometer. If the reading of the pressure gage is 370 kPa, determine the gage pressure of the gasoline line.
Given:
  • Pgage = 370 kPa
  • Water column: 45 cm
  • Oil SG = 0.79, column: 50 cm
  • Mercury SG = 13.6, column: 22 cm
  • Gasoline SG = 0.70, column: 10 cm
  • ρwater = 1000 kg/m³
Formulas:
ρ = SG × ρwater
Pgas = Pgage + ρwg(0.45) – ρoilg(0.50) + ρHgg(0.22) – ρgasg(0.10)
Step-by-step:
1ρoil = 0.79 × 1000 = 790 kg/m³
2ρHg = 13.6 × 1000 = 13,600 kg/m³
3ρgas = 0.70 × 1000 = 700 kg/m³
4Pgas = 370,000 + (1000)(9.81)(0.45) – (790)(9.81)(0.50) + (13,600)(9.81)(0.22) – (700)(9.81)(0.10)
5Pgas = 370,000 + 4,414.5 – 3,874.95 + 29,351.52 – 686.7
6Pgas = 399,204.37 Pa ≈ 399.2 kPa
Gage pressure of gasoline line ≈ 399.2 kPa
Practice 2.5 – U-tube Manometer
Image: U-tube manometer connected between points A and B in a pipeline. Point B is 0.3 m higher than A. Manometer fluid density = 13,600 kg/m³.
A U-tube manometer measures the pressure difference between points A and B in a pipeline conveying water (ρ = 1000 kg/m³). The density of the manometer fluid is 13,600 kg/m³, and point B is 0.3 m higher than point A. Calculate the pressure difference.
Given:
  • ρwater = 1000 kg/m³
  • ρman = 13,600 kg/m³
  • Elevation difference = 0.3 m
Formulas:
PA – PB = ρg(b – a) + hg(ρman – ρ)
Step-by-step:
1Identify the manometer reading h and the elevation difference (b – a) = 0.3 m.
2Use the derived formula: PA – PB = ρg(0.3) + hg(13,600 – 1000)
3Substitute h from the manometer reading to get the final pressure difference.
Pressure difference = ρg(0.3) + hg(12,600)

Ch 3 Hydrostatic Force & Buoyancy

Resultant force on submerged plane surfaces, centre of pressure, and buoyancy (Archimedes’ principle).

📐 Key Formulas – Hydrostatics

FR = PC A = ρ g hC A
yP = yC + Ixc / (yC A)
hP = hC + Ixc / (hC A)
FB = ρf g Vdisplaced
W = ρbody g Vbody
  • hC = vertical depth of centroid
  • Ixc = second moment of area about centroidal axis
  • For rectangle: Ixc = bd³/12
  • For circle: Ixc = πR⁴/4
Example 3.1 – Vertical Circular Surface
Image: Circular plane surface, diameter 2 m, vertical, top edge 0.5 m below water surface.
A plane circular surface of diameter 2 m is vertical and its top edge is 0.5 m below the water surface. Find the magnitude of the force on one side and the depth of the centre of pressure.
Given:
  • Diameter D = 2 m → R = 1 m
  • Top edge depth = 0.5 m
  • hC = 0.5 + 1 = 1.5 m
  • ρwater = 1000 kg/m³
  • g = 9.81 m/s²
Formulas:
FR = ρ g hC A
hP = hC + Ixc / (hC A)
Ixc = πR⁴/4 (circle)
A = πR²
Step-by-step:
1A = π(1)² = 3.1416 m²
2FR = (1000)(9.81)(1.5)(3.1416) = 46,238 N ≈ 46.24 kN
3Ixc = π(1)⁴/4 = 0.7854 m⁴
4hP = 1.5 + 0.7854 / (1.5 × 3.1416) = 1.5 + 0.1667 = 1.667 m
FR = 46.24 kN · Centre of pressure at 1.667 m depth
Example 3.2 – Plywood Sheet (Concrete)
Image: Plywood sheet 1.22 m × 2.44 m, hinged at top, stop at bottom. Concrete depth 0.96 m, SG = 2.4.
A plywood sheet 1.22 m × 2.44 m is used to block poured concrete. The sheet is hinged at the top and a stop is nailed at the floor. Concrete is poured to a depth of 0.96 m. Specific gravity of concrete = 2.4. Calculate (a) the magnitude and location of the resultant force, and (b) the force on the stop.
Given:
  • Sheet width = 1.22 m, height = 2.44 m
  • Concrete depth h = 0.96 m
  • SGconcrete = 2.4 → ρ = 2400 kg/m³
  • hC = 0.96/2 = 0.48 m
  • g = 9.81 m/s²
Formulas:
FR = ρ g hC A
hP = hC + Ixc / (hC A)
Ixc = bd³/12 (rectangle)
Moment about hinge = FR × (hP) = Fstop × 0.96
Step-by-step:
1A = 1.22 × 0.96 = 1.1712 m²
2FR = (2400)(9.81)(0.48)(1.1712) = 13,235 N ≈ 13.24 kN
3Ixc = (1.22)(0.96)³/12 = 0.0899 m⁴
4hP = 0.48 + 0.0899 / (0.48 × 1.1712) = 0.48 + 0.16 = 0.64 m
5Moment about hinge: FR × (0.96 – hP) = Fstop × 0.96
613,235 × (0.96 – 0.64) = Fstop × 0.96 → Fstop = 4,412 N ≈ 4.41 kN
FR = 13.24 kN at hP = 0.64 m · Force on stop = 4.41 kN
Example 3.3 – Circular Window (Cruise Ship)
Image: Circular window 30 cm diameter, midpoint 5 m below water surface. Sea water SG = 1.025.
A room in a cruise ship has a 30-cm-diameter circular window. The midpoint of the window is 5 m below the water surface. Determine the hydrostatic force acting on the window and the pressure centre. Take SGsea = 1.025.

Example 3.4 – Vertical Trapezoidal Gate
Image: Vertical trapezoidal gate. Top width 1 m, bottom width 3 m, height 2 m. Top edge at 5 m depth.
For the vertical trapezoidal gate, determine the resultant force and centre of pressure.
Given:
  • Top width a = 1 m, bottom width b = 3 m, height h = 2 m
  • Top edge at 5 m depth
  • ρwater = 1000 kg/m³, g = 9.81 m/s²
Formulas:
hC = 5 + [2(2a + b)] / [3(a + b)]
A = (a + b)h / 2
FR = ρ g hC A
Io = h³(a² + 4ab + b²) / [36(a + b)]
hP = hC + Io / (hC A)
Step-by-step:
1hC = 5 + [2(2×1 + 3)] / [3(1 + 3)] = 5 + [10/12] = 5.833 m
2A = (1 + 3)(2)/2 = 4 m²
3FR = (1000)(9.81)(5.833)(4) = 228,900 N ≈ 228.9 kN
4Io = 2³(1² + 4×1×3 + 3²) / [36(1 + 3)] = 8(1 + 12 + 9)/144 = 176/144 = 1.222 m⁴
5hP = 5.833 + 1.222 / (5.833 × 4) = 5.833 + 0.0524 = 5.885 m
FR = 228.9 kN · Centre of pressure at 5.885 m
Practice Buoyancy – Steel Block in Lake
Image: Crane lowering a 1-m diameter spherical steel block into a lake. ρsteel = 8050 kg/m³.
A crane is used to lower weights into a lake. Determine the tension in the rope due to a 1-m-diameter spherical steel block (ρ = 8050 kg/m³) when (a) suspended in air, (b) completely immersed in water.

  • D = 1 m → R = 0.5 m
  • ρsteel = 8050 kg/m³
  • ρwater = 1000 kg/m³
  • g = 9.81 m/s²
Formulas:
V = (4/3)πR³
W = ρsteel g V
FB = ρwater g V
T = W – FB (when immersed)
Step-by-step:
1V = (4/3)π(0.5)³ = 0.5236 m³
2W = (8050)(9.81)(0.5236) = 41,344 N ≈ 41.34 kN

4FB = (1000)(9.81)(0.5236) = 5,136 N ≈ 5.14 kN
5(b) Immersed: T = 41,344 – 5,136 = 36,208 N ≈ 36.21 kN

Practice Volume & Density from Weight
Image: Irregular body weighed in air and water using a spring scale.
The volume and average density of an irregularly shaped body are to be determined. The body weighs 7200 N in air and 4790 N in water. Determine the volume and the density of the body.
Given:
  • Wair = 7200 N
  • Wwater = 4790 N
  • ρwater = 1000 kg/m³
  • g = 9.81 m/s²
Formulas:
FB = Wair – Wwater
FB = ρwater g V
ρbody = Wair / (g V)
Step-by-step:

Ch 4 Mass Continuity & Bernoulli

Discharge, mean velocity, conservation of mass, and Bernoulli’s equation with applications (Pitot tube, Venturi meter, orifices).

📐 Key Formulas – Continuity & Bernoulli

Q = A V   (discharge = area × mean velocity)
ρ1 A1 V1 = ρ2 A2 V2   (continuity)
A1 V1 = A2 V2   (incompressible)
p1/ρg + V1²/2g + z1 = p2/ρg + V2²/2g + z2
V = √(2gh)   (Torricelli / Pitot)
Q = A1/√[(A1/A2)² – 1] · √[2gh(ρman/ρ – 1)]   (Venturi)
  • Patm = 101.3 kPa = 1 atm
  • ρwater = 1000 kg/m³
  • g = 9.81 m/s²
Example 4.1 – Garden Hose & Nozzle
Image: Garden hose filling a 10-gallon bucket. Hose inner diameter = 2 cm, nozzle exit diameter = 0.8 cm. Fill time = 50 s.
A garden hose attached with a nozzle is used to fill a 10-gal bucket. The inner diameter of the hose is 2 cm, and it reduces to 0.8 cm at the nozzle exit. If it takes 50 s to fill the bucket, determine (a) the volume and mass flow rates of water through the hose, and (b) the average velocity of water at the nozzle exit.
Given:
  • Volume = 10 gal = 10 × 3.7854 L = 37.854 L
  • Time = 50 s
  • Hose diameter = 2 cm = 0.02 m
  • Nozzle diameter = 0.8 cm = 0.008 m
  • ρwater = 1000 kg/m³ = 1 kg/L
Formulas:
Q = V / t
ṁ = ρ Q
Q = A V → V = Q / A
A = πD²/4
Step-by-step:
1Q = 37.854 L / 50 s = 0.757 L/s
2ṁ = (1 kg/L)(0.757 L/s) = 0.757 kg/s
3Anozzle = π(0.004)² = 5.027×10⁻⁵ m²
4Vnozzle = Q / A = 0.757×10⁻³ / 5.027×10⁻⁵ = 15.1 m/s
Q = 0.757 L/s · ṁ = 0.757 kg/s · Vnozzle = 15.1 m/s
Example 4.2 – Maximum Jet Height (Bernoulli)
Image: Hose with water main at 400 kPa gage. Child covers outlet, jet shoots upward.
Water is flowing from a hose attached to a water main at 400 kPa gage. A child places his thumb to cover most of the hose outlet, causing a thin jet of high-speed water to emerge. If the hose is held upward, what is the maximum height that the jet could achieve?
Given:
  • P1,gage = 400 kPa
  • ρwater = 1000 kg/m³
  • g = 9.81 m/s²
  • V1 ≈ 0 (inside hose)
  • V2 = 0 (at top of trajectory)
Formulas:
p1/ρg + V1²/2g + z1 = p2/ρg + V2²/2g + z2
z2 = P1,gage / (ρg)
Step-by-step:
1Bernoulli: P1/ρg = Patm/ρg + z2
2z2 = (P1 – Patm)/ρg = P1,gage/ρg
3z2 = 400,000 / (1000 × 9.81) = 40.8 m
Maximum jet height = 40.8 m

The velocity in branch CE is 2.5 m/s. Find (a) volume flow rate in AB, (b) velocity in BC, (c) velocity in CD, (d) diameter of CE.

A = πD²/4
QAB = QBC = QCD + QCE

Ch 5 Momentum – Jet Striking a Plate

Force exerted by a jet on stationary and moving flat plates, inclined plates, curved vanes, and pipe bends.

📐 Key Formulas – Momentum

F = ṁ (vout – vin)
F = ρ A (v – u)²   (jet on moving flat plate, normal)
F = ρ A (v – u)² cos θ   (inclined plate, θ from jet direction)
Fx = ṁ (vx,out – vx,in)
Fy = ṁ (vy,out – vy,in)
R = √(Rx² + Ry²)
  • v = jet velocity, u = plate velocity
  • θ = angle of inclination
  • For stationary plate: u = 0
Practice 5.1 – Flat Plate (Stationary & Moving)
Image: Flat plate struck normally by a water jet. Jet diameter = 50 mm, velocity = 18 m/s.
A flat plate is struck normally by a jet of water 50 mm in diameter with a velocity of 18 m/s. Calculate: (a) the force on the plate when it is stationary, (b) the force on the plate when it moves in the same direction as the jet with a velocity of 6 m/s.
Given:
  • d = 50 mm = 0.05 m
  • v = 18 m/s
  • u = 6 m/s (for part b)
  • ρwater = 1000 kg/m³
Formulas:
A = πd²/4
F = ρ A v²   (stationary)
F = ρ A (v – u)²   (moving)
Step-by-step:
1A = π(0.05)²/4 = 1.9635×10⁻³ m²
2(a) F = 1000 × 1.9635×10⁻³ × (18)² = 636.2 N
3(b) F = 1000 × 1.9635×10⁻³ × (18 – 6)² = 1000 × 1.9635×10⁻³ × 144 = 282.7 N
(a) F = 636.2 N · (b) F = 282.7 N

Practice 5.5 – 90° Elbow (Pipe Bend)
Image: 90° elbow directing water upward. Flow rate = 25 kg/s, diameter = 10 cm, elevation difference = 35 cm.
A 90° elbow is used to direct water flow at a rate of 25 kg/s in a horizontal pipe upward. The diameter of the entire elbow is 10 cm. The elbow discharges water into the atmosphere. Example 5.2 – Curved Vane (Jet Deflection)
Image: Curved vane deflecting jet through 60°. Inlet velocity = 30 m/s, outlet = 25 m/s, discharge = 0.8 kg/s.
A jet of water from a nozzle is deflected through an angle θ = 60° from its original direction by a curved vane which it enters tangentially without shock with a mean velocity of 30 m/s and leaves with a mean velocity of 25 m/s. If the discharge ṁ from the nozzle is 0.8 kg/s, calculate the magnitude and direction of the resultant force on the vane if the vane is stationary.
Given:
  • v1 = 30 m/s
  • v2 = 25 m/s
  • θ = 60°
  • ṁ = 0.8 kg/s
Formulas:
Fx = ṁ(v1 – v2 cos θ)
Fy = ṁ(v2 sin θ – 0)
R = √(Fx² + Fy²)
φ = tan⁻¹(Fy/Fx)
Step-by-step:
1Fx = 0.8(30 – 25 cos 60°) = 0.8(30 – 12.5) = 14 N
2Fy = 0.8(25 sin 60° – 0) = 0.8 × 21.65 = 17.32 N
3R = √(14² + 17.32²) = √(196 + 300) = 22.3 N
4φ = tan⁻¹(17.32/14) = 51.0°
R = 22.3 N at 51.0° to the x-axis
Practice 5.7 – Cart Moving with Jet
Image: Water jet (15 m/s) strikes cart moving at 5 m/s. Mass flow rate = 25 kg/s.
Water accelerated by a nozzle to 15 m/s strikes the vertical back surface of a cart moving horizontally at a constant velocity of 5 m/s in the flow direction. The mass flow rate of water is 25 kg/s. After the strike, the water stream splatters off in all directions in the plane of the back surface. Determine the force that needs to be applied on the brakes of the cart to prevent it from accelerating. If this force were used to generate power instead of wasting it on the brakes, determine the maximum amount of power that can be generated.
Given:
  • Vjet = 15 m/s
  • Vcart = 5 m/s
  • ṁ = 25 kg/s
Formulas:
Relative velocity = Vjet – Vcart
F = ṁ(Vjet – Vcart)
P = F × Vcart
Step-by-step:
1Relative velocity = 15 – 5 = 10 m/s
2F = 25 × 10 = 250 N
3P = 250 × 5 = 1,250 W = 1.25 kW
Brake force = 250 N · Maximum power = 1.25 kW
Practice 5.8 – Plate on Frictionless Track
Image: Horizontal 5-cm water jet (18 m/s) strikes vertical plate of mass 1000 kg on frictionless track.
A horizontal 5-cm-diameter water jet with a velocity of 18 m/s impinges normally upon a vertical plate of mass 1000 kg. The plate is held in a nearly frictionless track and is initially stationary. When the jet strikes the plate, the plate begins to move in the direction of the jet. The water always splatters in the plane of the retreating plate. Determine (a) the acceleration of the plate when the jet first strikes it (time 0), (b) the time it will take for the plate to reach a velocity of 9 m/s, and (c) the plate velocity 20 s after the jet first strikes the plate. Assume the velocity of the jet relative to the plate remains constant.
Given:
  • d = 5 cm = 0.05 m
  • Vjet = 18 m/s
  • mplate = 1000 kg
  • ρwater = 1000 kg/m³
Formulas:
A = πd²/4
F = ρA(Vjet – u)²
a = F / m
t = ∫ du / a (numerical or iterative)
Step-by-step:
1A = π(0.05)²/4 = 1.9635×10⁻³ m²
2(a) At t=0, u=0: F = 1000 × 1.9635×10⁻³ × (18)² = 636.2 N
3a = F/m = 636.2 / 1000 = 0.636 m/s²
4(b) Use iterative/numerical method to find t when u = 9 m/s.
5(c) At t = 20 s, use numerical integration or approximation.
(a) a = 0.636 m/s² · (b) & (c) require numerical solution
