Ch 2 Manometer

Pressure measurement using U-tube and differential manometers. Trace pressure through different fluids: down = add ρgh, up = subtract ρgh.

📐 Key Formulas – Manometer

P2 = P1   (same level, same continuous fluid)
P = Patm + ρgh   (pressure increases with depth)
Pgage = ρgh   ·   Pabs = Pgage + Patm
ρ = SG × ρwater   (ρwater = 1000 kg/m³)
1 atm = 101.325 kPa = 1.01325 bar
  • Going down → add ρgh
  • Going up → subtract ρgh
  • Ignore air columns (ρair ≈ 0)

Resultant force on submerged plane surfaces, centre of pressure, and buoyancy (Archimedes’ principle).

📐 Key Formulas – Hydrostatics

FR = PC A = ρ g hC A
yP = yC + Ixc / (yC A)
hP = hC + Ixc / (hC A)
FB = ρf g Vdisplaced

A water jet of velocity Vj impinges normal to a flat plate which moves to the right at velocity Vc. Find the force required to keep the plate moving at constant velocity if the jet density is 1000 kg/m³. The jet area is 3 cm², and Vj and Vc are 20 and 15 m/s.
Given:
  • Vj = 20 m/s, Vc = 15 m/s
  • A = 3×10⁻⁴ m², ρ = 1000 kg/m³
Formulas:
F = ρ A (Vj – Vc)²
Step-by-step:
1Relative velocity = 20 – 15 = 5 m/s
2F = 1000 × 3×10⁻⁴ × 25 = 7.5 N
Force required = 7.5 N

Example 5.2 – Curved Vane (Jet Deflection)
Image: Curved vane deflecting jet through 60°. Inlet velocity = 30 m/s, outlet = 25 m/s, discharge = 0.8 kg/s.
A jet of water from a nozzle is deflected through an angle θ = 60° from its original direction by a curved vane which it enters tangentially without shock with a mean velocity of 30 m/s and leaves with a mean velocity of 25 m/s. If the discharge ṁ from the nozzle is 0.8 kg/s, calculate the magnitude and direction of the resultant force on the vane if the vane is stationary.
Given:
  • v1 = 30 m/s, v2 = 25 m/s
  • θ = 60°, ṁ = 0.8 kg/s
Formulas:
Fx = ṁ(v1 – v2 cos θ)
Fy = ṁ(v2 sin θ)
R = √(Fx² + Fy²)
φ = tan⁻¹(Fy/Fx)
Step-by-step:
1Fx = 0.8(30 – 25 cos 60°) = 0.8(30 – 12.5) = 14 N
2Fy = 0.8(25 sin 60°) = 17.32 N
3R = √(14² + 17.32²) = 22.3 N
4φ = tan⁻¹(17.32/14) = 51.0°
R = 22.3 N at 51.0° to the x-axis
Practice 5.5 – 90° Elbow (Pipe Bend)
Image: 90° elbow directing water upward. Flow rate = 25 kg/s, diameter = 10 cm, elevation difference = 35 cm.
A 90° elbow is used to direct water flow at a rate of 25 kg/s in a horizontal pipe upward. The diameter of the entire elbow is 10 cm. The elbow discharges water into the atmosphere. The elevation difference between the centres of the exit and the inlet is 35 cm. Determine (a) the gage pressure at the centre of the inlet, and (b) the anchoring force needed to hold the elbow in place.
Given:
  • ṁ = 25 kg/s, D = 0.1 m
  • Δz = 0.35 m, ρ = 1000 kg/m³
Formulas:
V = ṁ / (ρA)
P1,gage = ρg(z2 – z1)
FRx + P1A = –ṁV
FRz = ṁV
FR = √(FRx² + FRz²)
Step-by-step:
1V = 3.18 m/s
2P1,gage = 3.43 kPa
3FRx = –106.5 N, FRz = 79.5 N
4FR = 132.9 N ≈ 133 N
P1,gage = 3.43 kPa · FR ≈ 133 N
Practice 5.6 – Reducing Elbow (45°)
Image: Reducing elbow deflecting water upward by 45°. Inlet area = 150 cm², exit area = 25 cm². Flow rate = 30 kg/s. Elevation difference = 40 cm. Mass of elbow + water = 50 kg.
A reducing elbow is used to deflect water flow at a rate of 30 kg/s in a horizontal pipe upward by an angle θ = 45° from the flow direction while accelerating it. The elbow discharges water into the atmosphere. The cross-sectional area of the elbow is 150 cm² at the inlet and 25 cm² at the exit. The elevation difference between the centres of the exit and the inlet is 40 cm. The mass of the elbow and the water in it is 50 kg. Determine the anchoring force needed to hold the elbow in place.
Given:
  • ṁ = 30 kg/s
  • A1 = 0.015 m², A2 = 0.0025 m²
  • θ = 45°, Δz = 0.40 m, melbow = 50 kg
Formulas:
V1 = ṁ/(ρA1), V2 = ṁ/(ρA2)
P1/ρg + V1²/2g + z1 = P2/ρg + V2²/2g + z2
Fx = ṁ(V2cosθ – V1) + P1A1
Fy = ṁ(V2sinθ) – Welbow
Step-by-step:
1V1 = 2 m/s; V2 = 12 m/s
2P1 = 73,968 Pa
3Fx = 30(12cos45° – 2) + 73,968(0.015) = 1,304 N
4Fy = 30(12sin45°) – 50×9.81 = –235.9 N
5FR = √(1,304² + 235.9²) = 1,325 N
Anchoring force FR ≈ 1,325 N
